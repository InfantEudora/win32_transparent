# Chasm design notes

A top-down colony sim in the spirit of Anno, low poly, built on a Townscaper-style irregular grid
instead of squares or hexes. This page holds the game's premise and the rules that cut across every
plan, then indexes the plans. Talked through 2026-10-04; nothing is built yet.

Reference art is in `art_source/chasm/`: a screenshot of the *A Little Age* demo (the look and the
zoom), a mashup of that screenshot with the chasm cut into it (the layout), and a painted concept
(the mood of the chasm itself).

**The first goal is the building mechanic, not a game.** Grid, terrain, painting and procedural
buildings get tested on their own before any gameplay is put on top of them.

---

## The world

**One valley, split down the middle by a chasm.** The chasm is the centrepiece and has to read as
enormous: its walls are many times taller than any building, with layered rock, waterfalls where
rivers meet the rim, and mist low down. Standing in it are shards, columns and terraces of the old
ground at lower levels, which can be built on and reached.

**The biomes frame it.** Mountains to the north, where the chasm ends in frozen ground that cannot
be crossed, so the two halves meet only across the chasm itself. Swamp and desert to the south.
Most of the rest is buildable land.

**Getting things up, down and across is the game's geography.** Only zeppelins cross the chasm and
reach the **islands** in its middle; winches on the rim reach the **balconies** on its walls (both
are terraces); bridges cross rivers, never
the chasm. The chasm is a logistics problem, not a backdrop. See `gameplay_plan.md` (2026-10-05).

## Rules that hold across every plan

### Scale and the camera: one zoom range, one level of detail

**A Little Age's screenshot is at its MAXIMUM zoom-out, and so is ours.** The camera never pulls
back further than that; there is no strategic map view drawn from the same scene. **Our map is
about 3x3 of those screens, roughly 100,000 fine cells.** That is the ballpark every plan sizes
against; it changes only if playtime later asks for a longer or shorter map.

**There is one level of detail.** Everything is drawn the same way at every zoom, and there are no
simplified far versions of chunks, buildings or forests. That makes the rendering budget concrete:
**one full screen of the densest content at maximum zoom-out** — a town, its fields and the forest
around it — has to draw at frame rate. Every plan that adds geometry is checked against that view.

### No textures: one palette

**Every mesh uses a single palette texture**, and a vertex's UV picks a colour cell in it. Nothing
is textured in the usual sense, and generated geometry needs no texture work at all. The look comes
from flat shading, lighting and shadow, as in A Little Age.

**Palette rows are a cheap variation axis.** If the palette holds a row per biome or season, desert
against temperate is a per-vertex row choice, and winter can be a uniform that shifts the row.
Keep that in mind when laying the palette out, even before seasons exist.

**Where it is (built in step 4):** `assets/textures/palette.png`, 32 x 16 cells of 8 pixels (widened from 16 columns for the props and buildings) - a row
per biome (temperate, desert, frozen, swamp; rows 4-7 the SEASONS - spring, autumn, autumn's red leaves, winter - each a copy of temperate, which is summer, and row 8 summer kept, written by the app, `seasons_plan.md`; row 15 the EFFECTS row - mist, foam, chimney smoke and a warm house's lit windows, the same in every biome; the rest spare), a column per material. `Palette.h`
names the rows and columns. **The PNG is the source - edit it in any paint program**, one flat
colour per cell; `tools/make_palette.py` only wrote the first one and refuses to overwrite it
without `--force`. The background colour is read from the palette's haze cell, so it follows an
edit too.

### Lighting: one sun and the sky

A high sun from the north-west (shadows fall short and down-right, as in the reference) and the
renderer's **ambient hemisphere** for the sky - a cool bright sky over a dim warm ground, which is
what keeps a shadow blue and readable. Tuned by measurement against the A Little Age screenshot,
not by eye: lit ground about 1.05x its palette colour, shade about 0.7x. No tone mapping and no
post-processing; with these levels nothing clips. If a later look needs either, it goes into core.

### Generated, not modelled

**Nearly every asset is generated by code**: the ground, the cliffs, the fields and the buildings.
**Only some props are modelled and placed** — trees first — and they use the same palette texture.

### The world is a seed plus edits

The grid, the terrain and every generated mesh follow from the world seed, so **none of them is ever
saved**. What a save holds is the seed, what the player painted, and the simulation's state
(colonists, stocks, construction). Anything that can be derived again is derived again.

### Replay from a saved state, from day one

Archer's replays are bit-exact, and that has paid for itself in debugging
(`docs/replay_determinism_plan.md`). Chasm does the same, with one addition: a run starts **from a
saved state**, not only from a fresh level, so a problem seen two hours into a game can be cut out
and replayed on its own.

So **save and restore come before gameplay**, not after it, and so does a per-tick state hash like
archer's `.trace`. A replay is a save plus an input recording (`core/InputRecording.h`).

- **World generation uses its own `RRandom` instance** (`Generate(seed)`), never the shared one, so
  that generating, regenerating or previewing a world never shifts the simulation's stream.
- **Generation gets a pinned hash per seed**, checked by the app's built-in checks (below), so an
  accidental change to the generator is caught immediately, the way `archer_test.trace` catches a
  change to archer. Same-machine bit-exactness is the bar, as for archer, in debug and release
  alike.

**Built (step 6).** A save is `saves/<name>.json`: the grid settings and what was painted, nothing
generated (`ChasmSave.h`). **Since step 10 the chasm itself is generated from the seed**, so a save
made before 2026-10-05 - which carried the hand-drawn feature lines - loads onto a different world:
its `features` are ignored, its world hash no longer matches, and its paint goes back through the
rules, so whatever no longer stands on flat dry plateau is refused rather than left invalid. An input recording's start state IS a save, so F9 records from the
current game and F10 / `input_replay` puts the game back there and replays. **The player's
commands are in the recording, not just their input**: a click becomes a zone command by way of
the camera, which is view and is not replayed, so `SIM_CMD_FLAG_RECORD` (core) writes the command
itself into the recording and applies it on replay at its tick - and drops live ones meanwhile.
**The replay test is retired** (the user, 2026-10-08): `tools/chasm_replay_test.py` and its
`saves/replay_test_*.json` are gone - its village sat at fixed coordinates on a map that keeps
changing. ONE new replay is to be made once the mapping work settles (by the agent on the rivers).
Until then a rule change is checked by hand, or by replaying a recording of its own and comparing
replay against replay (`replay_trace`).

### Rules and view are split

As in archer and bomber: **the rules own topology, terrain levels, painted zones and the
simulation**, and are deterministic. **The view owns the meshes**, rebuilt from the rules whenever
something changes. Mesh building is view-only, so it can run on `core/BackgroundWork.h` without any
effect on determinism; its results never flow back into the rules.

### Checks live in the app, where they can be seen

**Archer and bomber test their rules in separate engine-free builds (`make rules`). Chasm does
not.** The checks are built into the app and run as commands in it, so a failure can be looked at
in the same window it was found in: a folded quad is highlighted where it sits, not reported as a
line number.

- **Every check is a command.** Regenerating the grid, generating terrain, and running the checks
  are each one command, reachable from a debug panel and over MCP, so the person at the desk and
  an agent run exactly the same thing. A check reports pass or fail plus enough to find the
  failure (which vertex, which cell), and the debug view can jump the camera to it.
- **Every generated stage can be drawn.** The grid as lines, coarse and fine; vertex valence;
  quad squareness as colour; pinned chains; terrain levels; zones. Each is a toggle on the debug
  view, layered over the normal scene.
- **Both are debug-build only**, under `#ifdef DEBUG` (`-DDEBUG` is already set by `CONFIG=debug`
  in `engine.mk`). A release or ship exe carries none of it.
- **Fewer rules than archer.** Archer's rules are a platformer's physics and every edge case of
  it; Chasm's are mostly generation and bookkeeping. Check what can silently go wrong - topology,
  determinism, terrain levels agreeing with the pinned features - and let the visual debug views
  cover the rest.

## The wiki: what the game does today

The plans below are the reasons and the history, and they still hold decisions that were reversed
later. **`../wiki/` is the present tense**: a short page per thing in the game (the woodcutter, a
store, thirst), what it does now, what it touches and what is planned for it, built into one
clickable `build/wiki.html` by `python apps/chasm/tools/chasm_wiki.py`. Figures on a page are
constants read from the code, so a retune keeps them right on its own. A change to what a mechanic
does updates its page (`../wiki/README.md`). Started 2026-10-09 with the woodcutter's loop.

### Later, agreed but not yet planned

- **A buildable overlay, in every build.** With a tool in hand, the ground round the cursor tinted by

### Props: modelled, palette-coloured

Trees, rocks, stumps, logs and bushes are modelled - the exception "Generated, not modelled" makes
room for - in `art_source/chasm/chasm_props.blend`, one object per asset in its "Export" collection,
exported to `assets/meshes/chasm_props.glb` (Collection properties > Exporters > Export, one click).
Every face's UVs sit on a palette cell centre in row 0; the game draws them with its own palette
material and the GLB carries no image, so `palette.png` stays the one place colours live. Origins
at the centre of the base; bottoms that would be in the ground are left off and sink 0.08 below it.
15 assets, 13-118 triangles each, built for instancing. Previews in `art_source/chasm/previews/`.
Stump tops and log ends borrow the path colour for now - every column is taken; a cut-wood colour
needs the palette to grow again.
